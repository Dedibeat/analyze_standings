A parentheses sequence is a string composed of the characters ( and ). A parentheses sequence s1,2,...,m
is defined as valid if it satisfies one of the following conditions:
• s is empty.
• s1 = (, sm = ), and s2,3,...,m−1 is valid.
• There exists a position k(1 ≤ k < n) such that s1,2,...,k is valid, and sk+1,k+2,...,m is valid.
Two positions i, j(1 ≤ i < j ≤ m) in the parentheses sequence s1,2,...,m are said to be matched if and only
if both si,i+1,...,j and si+1,i+2,...,j−1 are valid.
Given an integer sequence of length n, a1 , a2 , . . . , an , where n is guaranteed to be even. We construct
a new integer sequence c1 , c2 , . . . , cn through a valid parentheses sequence b1 , b2 , . . . , bn , such that
∀1 ≤ i ≤ n, ci = api , where pi is the position matched with position i in b1,2,...,n .
Find a valid parentheses sequence b1,2,...,n such that the lexicographical order of c1,2,...,n is minimized. If
there are multiple solutions, output any one of them.

Input
The first line contains an integer T (1 ≤ T ≤ 2.5 × 105 ), indicating the number of test cases.
For each test case:
The first line contains an integer n(1 ≤ n ≤ 5 × 105 ), indicating the length of the sequence a, and it is
guaranteed that n is even.
The second line contains n space-separated integers, representing a1 , a2 , . . . , an (1 ≤ ai ≤ n).
It is guaranteed that the sum of n does not exceed 5 × 105 .

Output
For each test case, output a line containing a string of length n, representing b1,2,...,n .
