Long ago, in the land of Bitworld, there was a magic mirror. Whenever you looked into it, the index i of
an array element would be reflected as i ⊕ k for some magic key k. The wizard who owned this mirror
loved to play with sequences: sometimes he mirrored parts of the sequence using XOR, and sometimes he
asked for weighted sums of intervals.
Now the wizard gives you T sequences and a list of operations for each sequence. Your task is to process
them and report the results of his queries.
You are given an initial sequence A0 , A1 , . . . , AN −1 of length N . The value N is always a power of two
and satisfies N ≤ 218 .

Input
The first line contains a single integer T (1 ≤ T ≤ 2 × 105 ) — the number of test cases.
Each test case is given as:
One line with two integers N (1 ≤ N ≤ 218 , N is a power of two) and Q (1 ≤ Q ≤ 2 × 105 ) — the length
of the sequence and the number of operations.
One line with N integers A0 , A1 , . . . , AN −1 (1 ≤ Ai ≤ 1048576) — the initial sequence.
Each of the next Q lines describes one operation.
All operations are given on a left-closed, right-open interval [l, r) with 0 ≤ l < r ≤ N . The formats are:
1 l r k —– apply operation type 1 on the interval [l, r) with parameter k (0 ≤ k < N ).
2 l r —– apply operation type 2 on the interval [l, r).
