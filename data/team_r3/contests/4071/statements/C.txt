There is an unknown permutation p = (p1 , p2 , . . . , pn ) of the integers 1, 2, . . . , n. You are given m pieces
of information about this permutation.
Each piece of information consists of an interval [li , ri ] and a permutation qi,1 , qi,2 , . . . , qi,ri −li +1 of the
indices in this interval. It states that
pqi,1 < pqi,2 < · · · < pqi,ri −li +1 .
Your task is to find a permutation that satisfies all the given information and has the minimum possible
number of inversions. If no such permutation exists, output −1.
If several optimal permutations exist, output any of them.
The number of inversions of a permutation p is the number of pairs of indices (x, y) such that
1 ≤ x < y ≤ n and px > py .

Input
The first line contains a single integer T (1 ≤ T ≤ 106 ) — the number of test cases.
For each test case:
- The first line contains two integers n (1 ≤ n ≤ 106 ) and m (1 ≤ m ≤ 106 ) — the size of the permutation
and the number of constraints.
- Each of the next m lines describes a constraint. The i-th constraint starts with two integers li and ri
(1 ≤ li ≤ ri ≤ n), followed by ri − li + 1 integers qi,1 , qi,2 , . . . , qi,ri −li +1 , which form a permutation of the
integers from li to ri .

- If no satisfying permutation exists, print -1.

the number of inversions.
If there are multiple optimal permutations, print any of them.
