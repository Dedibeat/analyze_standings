Given three integers N , M , and K, consider all the unlabeled rooted trees with N vertices satisfying that
exactly M of these vertices have K children. Let A(N, M, K) be the number of such trees considering the
order of each vertex’s children.
Formally, two trees are considered different if the degrees of their roots are unequal or there exists a
number t such that the subtrees of the t-th child of the two trees are different.
Now you are given an integer N and Q pairs (M1 , K1 ), · · · , (MQ , KQ ). Find the values of
A(N, M1 , K1 ), · · · , A(N, MQ , KQ ).
Since the result may be large, output the answer modulo 998 244 353.

Input
The first line contains two integers N and Q (1 ≤ N ≤ 105 , 1 ≤ Q ≤ 106 ).
The following Q lines contain two integers each; the i-th line contains two integers Mi and Ki . It is
guaranteed that 0 ≤ Mi , Ki ≤ N .

Output
Output Q lines; the i-th line contains a single integer — A(N, Mi , Ki ) modulo 998 244 353.
