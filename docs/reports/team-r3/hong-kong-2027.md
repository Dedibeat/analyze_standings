## How hard should individual practice be?

SuperJ6's recommendation is a band where you solve **30–40% of new problems independently**. He suggests taking a hint when ideas have dried up, implementing afterward, and doing a weekly virtual in the target format. He gives no fixed rating offset. Mike Mirzayanov's original approximate *contest* curve gives about **76%** on a problem 200 points below the solver, **24%** at 200 above, and **30%** at about **147 above**. A displayed CF rating, a member's fitted contest level and a person's solo practice level can differ. [SuperJ6's guide](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/116371/) · [Codeforces difficulty announcement](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/62865)

Use the team's corrected November 5 fits as starting estimates. They count all rated problem opportunities, including skips. For a 50% level θ and fitted slope s, the problem rating at probability p is θ + s × ln((1−p)/p). The table rounds to available CF buckets for the first sessions. The starters have no recorded submission by that member on the named problem or contest in the November cache. Swap a recognized task for the next unseen one at the same rating. ## What to borrow from coaches and trainees

- **Mostafa Saad** assigns by a trainee's weak skill, difficulty and the value of the idea. His suggested mix is 10% easier, 20% around level, 60% slightly harder and 10% much harder. He allows 60–120 minutes on a normal problem. SuperJ6 would take a hint sooner when fresh ideas stop. For this team, use the common clock above for measurement, give a regional upsolve the longer block, and use mock openings for speed. Mostafa's 2016 trainees also asked to discuss accepted solutions and reported losing motivation on very hard assignments. End the repair block by having the backup explain the idea unaided. [Mostafa's release](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/79231) · [Sheet instructions](../../../../mostafa_sheet/info.md) · [Recorded trainee feedback](../../../../mostafa_sheet/mostafa.md)
- **Use trainee experience to choose the exercise.** In the sibling OI sheet, *Dreaming* (5.75, p3) had four independent ACs in four matching logs. One trainee describes two hours debugging during a mock. *City* (7.1, p4) had seven logs: two independent ACs, four assisted ACs, one partial attempt. An independent solver found little new, while an assisted solver singled out the reduction. Start Dedi with *Dreaming* and a brute checker, then have Tamir review *City* with him if time remains. For Tamir's greedy-check practice use *Robots* (6, p5 v1): ten of twelve logged ACs were independent, yet one trainee describes an incorrect assumption that made implementation harder. Ask for a proof and a falsifying test before coding. These are selected, self-reported experiences. The sheet levels order exercises within that collection. - **Michal Forišek** is the author identified in the repository's earlier research. His 2009 IRT paper estimates contestant ability and task difficulty/discrimination from outcomes. The 30–40% learning target comes from SuperJ6. Forišek's 2007 account of Slovak IOI preparation gives a training method to try here: graduated tasks and explicit arguments for correctness and complexity. Before Tamir gets the keyboard on a greedy or tree task, the solver gives the invariant and bound and the backup tries to break it. Follow a guided exercise with a fresh, tag-hidden problem to see whether the recognition transfers. ## One scorecard for the six mocks

Erdene starts the clearest opener while Tamir and Dedi split the unread statements. At 10 minutes, everyone names their best candidate. After 25 minutes stuck on an easy problem, get another reader. After two wrong submissions, the backup starts a cold read or reproduces the bug. After 45 minutes without progress, switch. At minute 200, pair on the best remaining problem and leave the third member free to finish independent work. Submit when there is a reason to believe the code passes.
